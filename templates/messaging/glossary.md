# Glossary

This document is the single reference for company-specific vocabulary and terminology. It has two sections: Controlled Vocabulary governs word choice (what to say and what not to say), and Terminology provides definitions for cross-cutting terms used across the messaging house.

## Messaging Blocks

### Controlled Vocabulary

[Instructions:
Define the controlled vocabulary for the company — specific terms to always use, terms to never use, and naming conventions for products, categories, and capabilities. This table governs word choice across all messaging and content.]

[Tips:
- Include product naming rules (capitalization, abbreviations, how to reference)
- List preferred terms alongside the alternatives you reject
- Cover category-level terminology (e.g., "exposure management" not "vulnerability scanning")
- This section absorbs what was previously "Naming and Terminology" in the profile doc]

[Format:
| Always use | Never use | Context |
|---|---|---|
| [term] | [rejected alternative] | [when/why] |]

### Terminology

[Instructions:
Define cross-cutting terms that carry company-specific meaning across the messaging house. Every term here should pass the litmus test: would a new writer joining the team encounter this term in customer-facing content and need to understand the company's specific definition to use it correctly?]

[Tips:
- Definitions are 1-3 sentences. Longer definitions suggest the term needs its own messaging section.
- Write in present tense, declarative voice. "[Term] is..." not "[Term] refers to..."
- Ground in the company's context. "[Term] is [Company]'s approach to..." not "[Term] is an industry practice that..."
- Every definition must trace to at least one messaging doc.
- Target range: 15-40 well-defined terms for most companies. More than 50 suggests standard terms are being included.]

[Format:
| Term | Definition | Context | See also |
|---|---|---|---|
| [term] | [1-3 sentence definition] | [where/when this term is used] | [related terms or docs] |]

## Writing Guidelines

- Controlled Vocabulary entries take precedence over all other word-choice guidance — if a term is specified in the Always use / Never use table, follow it exactly
- Terminology definitions are the canonical meaning of each term across the messaging house — all content must use terms consistent with these definitions
- When Controlled Vocabulary and Terminology both address the same term, Controlled Vocabulary governs the word form (what to say) and Terminology provides the meaning (what it means)
- Entries are sorted alphabetically within each table
- Do not include standard industry terms, product names (belong in portfolio.md), category names (belong in space.md), or universally understood acronyms
- When a term's usage shifts, update the definition here first — downstream docs inherit the change
