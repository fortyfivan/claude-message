# Industry Trend

A forward-looking analysis of a market shift, technology movement, or operational evolution that's reshaping how buyers think, buy, and operate. Positions the company as the voice that saw it coming and understands what it means.

## Structure

| Section | Purpose | Guidance |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Thesis | The trend in one argument | State the trend as a claim, not a question. "The security industry is consolidating around platforms" not "Is the security industry consolidating?" Take a position |
| Evidence | Proof the trend is real | Market data, analyst commentary, vendor moves, customer behavior changes, technology shifts. Layer multiple evidence types — no single source is sufficient for a trend claim |
| Drivers | What's causing this | The forces behind the trend — economic, technical, regulatory, behavioral. Go beyond surface observation to root cause |
| Current State | Where we are now | Map the landscape: who's moving, who's resisting, where adoption is concentrated, what the holdouts look like. Name names where appropriate |
| Implications | What this means for the reader | Different implications for different personas. What should a CISO do differently? What should a DevOps lead prepare for? Connect to personas in the messaging house |
| Predictions | Where this goes in 12-24 months | Grounded extrapolation. State predictions as "if X continues, then Y" — conditional, not prophetic. Include at least one contrarian prediction that challenges the consensus narrative |
| Your Move | How to position for the trend | Actionable guidance. This is where the company's approach can appear naturally — as an example of how to respond to the trend, not as the only response |

## Tone & Style

- **Voice:** Analytical, forward-looking, opinionated. An industry observer who's done their homework and isn't afraid to make predictions
- **Length:** 2,500-4,500 words
- **Altitude:** Executive and senior practitioner — this content gets shared in leadership meetings and board decks

Trend content ages. Date-stamp the analysis and write it to be verifiable — in 12 months, the reader should be able to check your predictions. That accountability is what separates authority from speculation.

## SEO/GEO Optimization

- Thesis should appear in H1 and first paragraph as a clear, quotable claim
- Each implication and prediction should be a standalone H2 section for AI extraction
- Include specific data points and named companies — these are search magnets
- Write the prediction sections to answer "what happens next in [topic]" queries directly

## Example

**Input:** Write an industry trend paper on the convergence of vulnerability management and exposure management

**Output:**
```markdown
# The End of Vulnerability Management as a Category

Vulnerability management is being absorbed. Not by a competitor, but by a
concept: exposure management. The distinction matters — and the vendors
that understand why will define the next decade of security operations.

## The Evidence Is Everywhere

Gartner introduced "Continuous Threat Exposure Management" (CTEM) in 2022.
By 2025, 60% of the analyst conversations in this space referenced CTEM
frameworks rather than traditional vulnerability management...

## What's Driving the Shift

Three forces are converging:

### The Attack Surface Outgrew the Scanner
Traditional vulnerability management assumes a known, bounded perimeter...

### Severity Scores Lost Their Meaning
CVSS was designed for a world where every vulnerability had roughly equal
probability of exploitation...

### Buyers Started Buying Outcomes, Not Tools
The procurement conversation shifted from "which scanner has the best
coverage" to "which platform reduces our actual exposure"...

[Continues with Current State → Implications → Predictions → Your Move]
```

## Quality Signals

| Criterion | Target | Red Flags |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Thesis Strength | Clear, debatable claim stated upfront — not a question, not a summary | "Here are some trends in security" — no argument, no position |
| Evidence Layering | Multiple evidence types (data, analyst, vendor moves, customer behavior) | Single-source argument or anecdotal evidence only |
| Causal Depth | Drivers explain root causes, not just symptoms | "Things are changing because of digital transformation" |
| Prediction Accountability | Predictions are specific enough to verify in 12 months | Vague predictions that can never be proven wrong |
| Balanced Perspective | Acknowledges counterarguments and resistance to the trend | One-sided narrative that ignores complications |
| Reader Agency | Reader leaves with specific actions, not just awareness | Paper informs but doesn't enable the reader to act |
