# Partner Story

A joint proof asset that demonstrates what two companies accomplish together — for mutual customers, for the market, and for each other's buyers. Partner stories validate the alliance relationship while expanding proof coverage into segments or use cases that neither company can claim alone.

## Who This Is For

Partner stories serve two distinct audiences simultaneously:

- **Mutual customers and prospects** — who want proof that the joint solution works in practice, not just on a joint solution brief
- **The partner's own audience** — who may not know your company and are evaluating whether the joint offering adds genuine value to what they already have

Write knowing both audiences will read it. Lead with the customer outcome. Let the partnership structure itself explain.

## Structure

| Section | Purpose | Guidance |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Headline | Lead with the joint outcome | Frame around what the mutual customer achieved — not the partnership itself |
| The Partnership | Establish what each company brings | One sentence each. What problem does each solve? What does the combination unlock? |
| Customer Snapshot | Establish the proof context | Who the mutual customer is, their environment, and why they needed both partners |
| The Challenge | Set the before state | What couldn't be solved without the joint capability — single-vendor approaches that failed or fell short |
| The Joint Solution | Describe how both capabilities worked together | Specific: which product did what, at what point, for whom |
| The Outcome | Deliver the joint proof | Quantitative results and the customer voice — attribute credit to the combination, not just one party |
| The Partnership Value | Close with the broader implication | What this proves is possible for other companies in similar situations |

## Tone & Style

- **Voice:** Collaborative and peer-level. Both companies are co-authors of this story.
- **Length:** 700-1,200 words
- **Altitude:** Match the audience — if this will live on a technology partner's site, write to their buyer persona

Neither company should be positioned as subordinate. Frame the relationship as complementary capability, not a hierarchy.

## Headline Patterns

- "[Mutual Customer] Achieved [Outcome] Using [Partner A] and [Partner B] Together"
- "How [Partner A] + [Partner B] Helped [Customer] Close a [N]% Asset Coverage Gap"
- "Joint Solution: How [Customer] Solved [Problem] With [Partner A] and [Partner B]"

Avoid headlines that only name one company — it signals the story is marketing for one party, not both.

## Partnership Setup

The opening paragraph must orient the reader on what each company does and why together is better than either alone. One paragraph max. Use plain language — the reader may know one partner deeply and have never heard of the other.

Example:
> [Company A] provides continuous asset visibility across hybrid IT environments. [Partner B] delivers endpoint protection across those same environments. For customers running both, the integration creates a real-time feedback loop: [Company A] identifies unprotected assets, [Partner B] deploys coverage. Neither system alone closes the gap automatically.

## Outcome Attribution

Joint stories require careful attribution. Be specific about which outcome came from which part of the joint solution:

- "[Company A] identified the unmanaged devices; [Partner B] brought them under protection within 48 hours."
- "The integration automated a workflow that previously required manual handoffs between two teams."

Avoid generic "together we achieved X" framing without describing the mechanism.

## Quote Strategy

Ideal partner story has three quote sources:

1. **The mutual customer** — on the joint outcome or the experience of using both together
2. **A leader from Company A** — on what the partnership unlocks for customers like this one
3. **A leader from Company B** — on the complementary fit and joint customer value

If partner leadership quotes aren't available, one strong customer quote is sufficient. Never fabricate or imply quotes.

## Example

**Input:** Write a partner story about a technology integration between an asset management platform and an endpoint security vendor, featuring a healthcare customer that used the integration to find and remediate unprotected devices.

**Output:**
```markdown
# How [Hospital System] Closed Its Endpoint Coverage Gap in 72 Hours — Using [Company A] and [Partner B] Together

**Industry:** Healthcare | **Size:** 18 hospitals, 40,000+ endpoints | **Region:** US Southeast

## Two Tools. One Closed Loop.

[Company A] gives security teams a continuously updated view of every asset in their environment. [Partner B] protects those assets with endpoint detection and response. For security teams running both, the integration does something neither tool does independently: it automatically identifies unprotected devices and triggers immediate coverage.

## The Customer

[Hospital System] operates 18 hospitals and a sprawling network of connected medical devices, administrative workstations, and clinical terminals. They had [Partner B] deployed across their known endpoint inventory — but "known" was the problem.

## The Challenge

When [Hospital System]'s security team ran their first correlation in [Company A], they discovered 4,200 devices in their environment that had no [Partner B] agent installed. Some were legacy clinical devices. Some were recently onboarded contractor laptops. Some were simply unknown.

Without [Company A], those devices weren't visible. Without [Partner B], they weren't protected. The manual process of identifying gaps and deploying agents was taking weeks per remediation cycle — too slow for a regulated healthcare environment.

## The Joint Solution

The [Company A] and [Partner B] integration enabled a real-time feedback loop:

- [Company A] ingested data from [Hospital System]'s network, cloud, and identity systems to build a complete asset inventory
- The integration flagged every device missing a [Partner B] agent directly within [Company A]'s console
- [Partner B]'s deployment API allowed the security team to initiate agent installation from within [Company A]'s workflow — without switching tools or opening a manual ticket

The entire identification-to-remediation workflow — previously measured in weeks — completed in 72 hours.

## The Outcome

**Before:** 4,200 unprotected devices. No automated path from discovery to remediation. Manual process taking 4-6 weeks per gap closure cycle.

**After:** All 4,200 devices brought under [Partner B] protection in 72 hours. Automated alerting now flags new unprotected devices within hours of discovery.

"We'd known there were gaps. We didn't know how many until [Company A] showed us, and we didn't have a way to close them fast until the integration with [Partner B]. That combination changed both problems at once." — [CISO, Hospital System]

## What This Proves

For healthcare organizations running [Partner B], [Company A] closes the gap between the endpoints [Partner B] knows about and the assets that actually exist in the environment. The integration doesn't require replacing anything — it makes what's already deployed more effective.

"Our customers who use both tools together consistently find coverage gaps they didn't know existed — and close them faster than they thought possible." — [VP Partnerships, Company A]
```

## Quality Signals

| Criterion | Target | Red Flags |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Joint Framing | Both partners clearly named and credited | Story reads as one company's marketing with the other mentioned briefly |
| Mechanism Clarity | Specific description of how both parts worked together | Vague "joint solution" without explaining the integration |
| Customer Centrality | Customer outcome is the headline and the anchor | Partnership announced before customer value is established |
| Attribution Specificity | Clear which outcome came from which capability | "Together we achieved X" without tracing what each party contributed |
| Dual Audience Fit | Works for both companies' prospect audiences | Written only for one partner's known buyer persona |
| Quote Coverage | Customer voice plus at least one partner leader | Only executive PR quotes, no customer proof |
