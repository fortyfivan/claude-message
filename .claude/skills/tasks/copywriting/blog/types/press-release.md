# Press Release

Formal announcement for media distribution with a structured journalistic format: dateline, lead paragraph, executive quotes, supporting details, and company boilerplate.

## Structure

| Section | Purpose | Guidance |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Headline | News hook in 8-12 words | Lead with the news, not the company name |
| Subheadline | Expand with key benefit | One sentence that adds context the headline omits |
| Dateline & Lead | Who, what, when, where, why | First paragraph answers all five; 2-3 sentences max |
| Executive Quote | Human voice on the news | Attribute to a named executive; convey vision, not features |
| Supporting Detail | Evidence and context | Metrics, customer impact, availability, integrations |
| Second Quote (optional) | Customer or partner voice | External validation; concrete outcome, not generic praise |
| Boilerplate | Company description | Standard "About [Company]" paragraph — reuse across releases |
| Media Contact | Press follow-up | Name, email, phone for media inquiries |

## Tone & Style

- **Voice:** Formal, neutral, factual — journalistic, not promotional
- **Length:** 400-600 words (excluding boilerplate and contact)
- **Altitude:** Accessible to general business audience; industry terms only when necessary

Press releases are read by journalists, analysts, and aggregators. Write for quotability — every sentence should be liftable into a news article without rewriting. Avoid superlatives, subjective claims, and marketing language. State facts; let the reader draw conclusions.

## Format Conventions

- **Dateline:** CITY, State/Country — Date — Format: `CITY, ST — Month Day, Year —`
- **Paragraph length:** 1-3 sentences per paragraph; no long blocks
- **Attribution:** "said [Name], [Title] at [Company]" — full title on first mention
- **Numbers:** Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10+
- **End mark:** Close with `###` centered on its own line
- **Embargo note:** If embargoed, add `EMBARGOED UNTIL: [date/time]` above the headline

## Example

**Input:** Write a press release announcing a new risk-based vulnerability prioritization feature

**Output:**
```markdown
## Content Brief
**Blog Type:** Press Release
**Target Audience:** Technology journalists, industry analysts, security practitioners
**Core Thesis:** [Company] launches risk-based vulnerability prioritization that reduces remediation noise by correlating vulnerabilities with asset context
**Market Context:** Security teams face alert fatigue from thousands of undifferentiated vulnerability findings
**Key Takeaways:** Contextual prioritization, reduced remediation workload, enterprise availability
**SEO Keywords:** vulnerability prioritization, risk-based remediation, asset context

## Blog Draft

**[Company] Launches Risk-Based Vulnerability Prioritization to Cut Through Alert Noise**

*New capability correlates vulnerability findings with asset context, helping security teams focus remediation on what actually threatens the business*

SAN FRANCISCO, CA — March 10, 2026 — [Company] today announced the general availability of Risk-Based Vulnerability Prioritization, a new capability that combines vulnerability scan data with asset intelligence to surface the issues that pose real risk to an organization.

Security teams routinely face tens of thousands of vulnerability findings with limited context on which ones matter. Traditional severity scores fail to account for asset criticality, network exposure, or compensating controls — forcing teams to either remediate blindly or accept unquantified risk.

"Vulnerability management has become a volume problem disguised as a security problem," said [Name], [Title] at [Company]. "By connecting vulnerability data to what we know about each asset — where it sits, what it does, who relies on it — we can finally answer the question security teams actually care about: which of these should I fix first?"

Risk-Based Vulnerability Prioritization evaluates each finding against asset criticality, network exposure, active exploitability, and existing compensating controls. The result is a continuously updated, prioritized queue that reflects the organization's specific risk posture rather than generic severity scores.

Early access customers have reported a 70% reduction in critical remediation queue volume without increasing risk exposure.

Risk-Based Vulnerability Prioritization is available today for all Enterprise plan customers.
```

## Quality Signals

| Criterion | Target | Red Flags |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Newsworthiness | Clear news hook in headline and lead | Reads like a blog post or marketing page |
| Quote Quality | Executive quote conveys vision, not features | Quote restates product capabilities verbatim |
| Factual Density | Specific numbers, dates, availability | Vague claims without supporting detail |
| Format Compliance | Dateline, attribution, boilerplate, contact, end mark | Missing structural elements |
| Tone Neutrality | Journalistic, factual, no superlatives | "Industry-leading," "best-in-class," or subjective praise |
| Quotability | Every sentence liftable by a journalist | Complex sentences requiring rewriting to cite |
