# Event Recap

Summarize the signal from a conference, summit, field event, or company-hosted event — extending its shelf life as SEO content, generating social fuel, and creating a record that positions the company as a thoughtful participant in the conversation that mattered.

## When to Use

- Post-conference recap (attended or exhibited at an industry event)
- Post-webinar or virtual summit recap
- Post-company event (customer day, partner summit, field event)
- Post-roundtable or executive briefing recap

## Structure

| Section | Purpose | Guidance |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Headline | Frame the event's significance | What was the event, and what was the one overarching signal? |
| Setting the Scene | Brief context on the event | What it was, who attended, why it mattered this year |
| The Big Themes | 2-4 themes that ran through the event | Each theme is a section — name it, describe it, ground it in specifics |
| What We Heard | Ground-level signal — conversations, questions, debates | What were practitioners actually saying in sessions, hallways, demo queues? |
| Our Read | Company perspective on what the signal means | What does this tell us about where the market is headed? |
| What's Next | Forward-looking close | How does this connect to upcoming work, trends to watch, or decisions practitioners should consider? |

## Tone & Style

- **Voice:** Informed participant. You were there. Write like someone sharing what they learned with a colleague who couldn't attend.
- **Length:** 800-1,400 words
- **Altitude:** Practitioner-friendly with executive summary potential — the first 150 words should be skimmable for an exec

This is a journalism-adjacent format. Lean on specifics: session names, speaker names (if approved), actual questions asked, real tensions in the room. Generic event coverage adds no value.

## Theme Development

Each theme section should follow this arc:
1. **Name the theme** — a headline-worthy claim, not a generic topic label
2. **Ground it** — which sessions, speakers, or conversations surfaced it?
3. **Tension** — where is the market still divided or unsure?
4. **Implication** — what should a practitioner do with this information?

Avoid: "AI was a major topic at the event." Write: "AI showed up in every session, but the real debate wasn't capability — it was governance. Half the room had deployed AI-assisted tools and wasn't sure how to audit them."

## What We Heard Section

This is the differentiating section. It moves the recap from "here's what happened" to "here's what people were actually thinking." Use:

- Direct (anonymized) quotes or paraphrases from attendees
- Questions asked during sessions that revealed real practitioner anxiety
- Debates or disagreements that emerged in panels or hallways
- Things that were conspicuously absent from the conversation

## Our Read

This is where the company's perspective lives. It should be a genuine point of view — not a product pitch. Connect the event themes to your market understanding. Disagree with consensus if your perspective differs. This section is what turns the recap from journalism into thought leadership.

## SEO/GEO Optimization

- Include the event name, year, and location in the first paragraph and meta description
- Target keywords: "[Event Name] recap," "[Event Name] key takeaways," "[Event Name] [year]," "[topic] conference [year]"
- Write section headers as standalone quotable claims — AI engines will extract them
- Publish within 48-72 hours of the event to capture peak search interest

## Quality Signals

| Criterion | Target | Red Flags |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Specificity | Named sessions, actual quotes, real debates | Generic "AI was everywhere" summary |
| POV | Clear company perspective on what the signal means | Pure journalism with no editorial voice |
| Timeliness | Published within 72 hours, references current context | Stale recaps without time-relevant framing |
| Theme Quality | 2-4 distinct, named themes with tension | One giant "here's everything that happened" section |
| Reader Value | Someone who wasn't there learns something real | Could have been written without attending |
| SEO Optimization | Event name, year, and keywords in headers and meta | No searchable terms in the first 100 words |
