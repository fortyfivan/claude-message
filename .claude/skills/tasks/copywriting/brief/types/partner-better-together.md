# Partner Better Together

An external-facing document that articulates the joint value proposition between your company and a specific partner — what the combination unlocks for mutual customers, why each company brings something the other doesn't, and what the combined offer looks like in practice. This is a customer-facing asset designed to help partners co-sell and help prospects understand the integrated value before they commit to either solution.

## Partner Types

This brief adapts to the partner relationship. Identify the partner type before writing — it determines the framing, the value narrative, and who the primary reader is.

### Channel Partner
A VAR, MSP, or systems integrator that packages and delivers your solution as part of their service offering. The brief frames how customers get the best of both: the partner's domain expertise and delivery capability combined with your product's core functionality.

**Primary reader:** Customers who buy through this partner and want to understand what they're getting  
**Value frame:** Trusted delivery + proven technology = better outcomes than either alone  
**Proof emphasis:** Customer references that came through this channel, partner delivery outcomes

### Reseller Partner
A company that sells your product to their customer base, often alongside complementary offerings. The brief explains why this combination makes sense commercially and for the buyer — what the reseller's customer base gets by choosing you through their trusted vendor.

**Primary reader:** The reseller's existing customers who encounter your product in their catalog  
**Value frame:** Your product + their existing relationship and commercial model = lower friction, integrated experience  
**Proof emphasis:** Customers who bought through the reseller and benefited from the integrated relationship

### Technology Partner
A complementary software or platform vendor whose product integrates with yours. The brief explains how the integration works, what it enables, and why deploying both together is better than either alone.

**Primary reader:** Customers evaluating both products or already using one and considering the other  
**Value frame:** Combined capability the integration unlocks that neither product provides independently  
**Proof emphasis:** Integration-specific outcomes — metrics that only happened because both products worked together

### Ecosystem Partner
A company that participates in a broader platform ecosystem — contributing to or benefiting from the network effects of your platform or category. The brief frames the mutual amplification: each partner makes the other more valuable as the ecosystem grows.

**Primary reader:** Customers building on or operating within the shared ecosystem  
**Value frame:** Ecosystem membership, interoperability, and the platform effect  
**Proof emphasis:** Ecosystem-scale metrics, integration count, joint customer breadth

### Marketplace Partner
A company available through a cloud or procurement marketplace (AWS Marketplace, Azure Marketplace, Salesforce AppExchange, etc.). The brief frames the procurement simplicity, pre-validated integration, and consolidated billing or support advantages.

**Primary reader:** Buyers who discover and evaluate solutions through the marketplace  
**Value frame:** Discovery, procurement, deployment, and billing consolidated — lower friction, faster time to value  
**Proof emphasis:** Deployment speed, marketplace-specific metrics, consolidated support experience

## Structure

| Section | Purpose | Guidance |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Joint Headline | Frame the combined value | Lead with the customer outcome the partnership enables — not the partnership itself |
| The Partnership | What each company is and brings | One crisp paragraph on each company — written for readers who may know one but not the other |
| Why Together | What the combination unlocks | The specific capability, workflow, or outcome that requires both — the "why not just one" answer |
| How It Works | The joint solution in practice | For technology partners: the integration mechanism. For channel/reseller: the delivery model. For marketplace: the procurement path. |
| Customer Proof | Joint outcomes from real deployments | Quotes or metrics that only exist because the partnership was in place |
| Getting Started | How a prospect engages | Partner-appropriate CTA — contact a rep, request a joint demo, find us on the marketplace, reach the partner |

## Tone & Style

- **Voice:** Collaborative and peer-level. Both companies co-own this document.
- **Length:** 1-2 pages (500-900 words)
- **Altitude:** Decision-maker and evaluator — the person who will decide whether to engage both companies or just one

Neither company should read as the primary author. The joint framing should feel co-owned.

## Why Together Section

This is the proof of concept for the partnership. It must answer the skeptic's question: "Why can't I just use one of these?" The answer should be specific:

- For technology partners: Name the workflow or outcome that requires both products to work together
- For channel partners: Name what the partner's delivery and relationship enable that a direct sale doesn't
- For reseller partners: Name the commercial or operational advantage of buying through this relationship
- For ecosystem partners: Name the network effect or interoperability advantage
- For marketplace partners: Name the procurement, billing, or deployment advantage

Generic answers ("better together," "complementary capabilities") don't pass the test. Specific joint outcomes do.

## Example

**Input:** Write a technology partner better together brief for an integration between an asset management platform and a SIEM vendor.

**Output:**
```markdown
# Complete Asset Context, Faster Threat Detection

When every security alert includes full asset context, your SOC team investigates faster, escalates smarter, and closes more incidents with the same headcount.

## [Company A]: The Asset Foundation

[Company A] continuously discovers and inventories every asset across managed and unmanaged devices, cloud infrastructure, and network infrastructure — correlating data from 700+ integrations into a single, authoritative asset record.

## [Partner B]: The Detection Layer

[Partner B] aggregates security events across the entire environment, applying behavioral analytics to surface threats that rule-based detection misses.

## What the Integration Unlocks

Every alert [Partner B] raises now includes the full asset context from [Company A]: the device's owner, last-seen security tool coverage, open vulnerabilities, cloud placement, and compliance status. Analysts don't leave the [Partner B] console to look up what the asset is — the information arrives with the alert.

The result: investigation time drops because context is immediate, and escalation decisions are grounded in complete asset data rather than partial information.

## How It Works

The [Company A] + [Partner B] integration connects via API. [Company A] continuously pushes updated asset records to [Partner B], enriching every event log with current asset attributes. Setup takes less than two hours. No new infrastructure required.

## What Joint Customers Find

"Before the integration, investigating an alert meant 20 minutes of cross-referencing tools to figure out what the asset was. Now I click the alert and the answer is right there." — [SOC Lead, Financial Services]

Average investigation time reduction across joint deployments: 40%.

## Getting Started

Already a [Partner B] customer? Connect [Company A] in under two hours — [link to integration guide].  
Evaluating both? [Request a joint demo →]
```

## Quality Signals

| Criterion | Target | Red Flags |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Partner Type Fit | Framing, value narrative, and CTA match the actual partner relationship type | Technology partner brief that reads like a channel brief |
| Joint Headline | Customer outcome in the headline — not the companies or the partnership | "Company A and Partner B: Better Together" (no outcome) |
| Why Together Specificity | Concrete description of what requires both — the joint mechanism | Generic "complementary capabilities" without naming the specific unlock |
| Audience Orientation | Written for the customer who knows one partner and needs to evaluate the other | Written for the partner's internal team rather than their customers |
| Proof Specificity | Outcomes that only exist because both were in place | Generic customer quotes that don't reference the joint use |
| CTA Appropriateness | Next step matches the partner type (marketplace link vs. joint demo vs. partner contact) | Generic "contact us" with no partner-specific path |
