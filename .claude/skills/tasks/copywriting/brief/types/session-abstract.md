# Session Abstract

A 100-150 word submission for conference sessions and event programs — tightly structured to frame a problem, scope the session, and promise specific attendee takeaways.

## Structure

| Section | Purpose | Guidance |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Title | Session name in 8-12 words | Convey the tension or question, not the answer |
| Problem Statement | Why this matters (1-2 sentences) | Frame the challenge the audience recognizes |
| Session Description | What will be covered (2-3 sentences) | Scope the content without giving it all away |
| Key Takeaways | What attendees leave with (3-4 bullets) | Specific, actionable outcomes — not vague promises |
| Speaker Bio | Credibility and relevance (2-3 sentences) | Connect expertise to the session topic |

## Tone & Style

- **Voice:** Authoritative, concise, problem-forward
- **Length:** 100-150 words for the abstract body (title and speaker bio are additional)
- **Altitude:** Match the event audience — practitioner events get tactical framing, executive events get strategic framing

Session abstracts compete for selection. Every word must earn its place. Lead with the problem the audience already feels, not a product pitch. Conference reviewers reject vendor pitches immediately — position the speaker as a practitioner sharing lessons, not a company selling solutions.

## Content Guidelines

- **No product names** in the abstract body — the session is about the problem and approach, not the product
- **No company positioning** — save that for the speaker bio
- **Specificity over scope** — "3 patterns for reducing MTTD by 40%" beats "best practices for detection and response"
- **Tension in the title** — pose a question, challenge an assumption, or name a contradiction
- **Takeaways must be concrete** — "A framework for..." or "How to evaluate..." not "Understanding the importance of..."

## Example

**Input:** Write a session abstract for a security conference talk on attack surface management

**Output:**
```markdown
## Brief Specification
**Type:** Session Abstract
**Target Audience:** Security practitioners at RSA Conference
**Objective:** Get the session accepted by demonstrating relevance and speaker expertise
**Key Message:** Continuous asset visibility eliminates the blind spots that attackers exploit
**Proof Points:** 15% average visibility gap statistic, Fortune 500 deployment example

## Content Outline
Title → Problem Statement → Session Description → Key Takeaways → Speaker Bio

## Brief Content

**You Can't Protect What You Can't See: Closing the Asset Visibility Gap**

Security teams defend environments they don't fully understand. On average, 15% of enterprise assets don't appear in any security tool — creating blind spots that auditors find and attackers exploit.

This session examines the root causes of asset visibility gaps and presents a practical framework for continuous asset intelligence. Drawing from deployments across Fortune 500 environments, we'll walk through how teams moved from periodic inventory snapshots to real-time visibility — and the operational impact on vulnerability management, compliance, and incident response.

**Attendees will leave with:**
- A diagnostic checklist for measuring their own visibility gap
- Three integration patterns for aggregating asset data across security and IT tools
- A 90-day roadmap for implementing continuous asset intelligence
- Metrics for reporting asset visibility to security leadership

**Speaker Bio:**
[Name] is [Title] at [Company] with [X] years in security operations and asset management. They have led asset intelligence programs at [notable organizations] and contributed to [relevant industry frameworks or publications].
```

## Quality Signals

| Criterion | Target | Red Flags |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Word Count | 100-150 words for abstract body | Over 150 words or padded to fill space |
| Problem Clarity | Audience recognizes the challenge immediately | Abstract problem statement or jargon-heavy framing |
| Takeaway Specificity | Each takeaway names a concrete deliverable | "Learn about..." or "Understand the importance of..." |
| Audience Hook | Title and opening create urgency | Generic title that could apply to any vendor |
| No Product Pitch | Zero product names or company positioning in body | Company name or product features in the abstract |
| Speaker Credibility | Bio connects expertise to the session topic | Generic bio not relevant to the session |
