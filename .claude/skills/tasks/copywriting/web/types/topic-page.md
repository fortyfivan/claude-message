# Topic Page

A long-form, SEO-optimized pillar page that owns a category-level topic — "What is [X]?", "[X] explained", or "[X] for [audience]" — and funnels organic traffic into product and solution consideration. Topic pages are educational by design. They answer the question the reader typed, then show why your approach matters.

## When to Use

- Owning a category search term the company wants to rank for
- Building a content hub around a topic cluster
- Creating GEO-optimized reference content that AI engines will cite
- Educating buyers who are in early research, before they know which vendor to consider

## What This Is Not

Topic pages are not product pages. The reader is here to learn, not to evaluate a purchase. Overt selling undermines the page's authority and search performance. The CTA connects the educational content to the product — it doesn't interrupt it.

## Structure

| Section | Purpose | Guidance |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Title / H1 | Clear statement of what this page covers | Match the search query — if people search "what is asset management," the H1 should be that phrase or close to it |
| Definition Block | Crisp answer to the core question | 2-3 sentences that would work as an AI-extracted answer. This is the featured snippet target. |
| Why It Matters | Stakes and context | Why does this topic matter right now? What happens when it's done poorly? |
| How It Works | Mechanism or process explanation | The "how" behind the topic — conceptual depth without vendor-specific framing |
| Key Components | The parts or dimensions of the topic | Structured subsections for each component — this is the middle of the page and should be comprehensive |
| Common Challenges | What makes this hard | Honest account of where organizations struggle — builds credibility and surfaces your differentiation |
| How [Company] Approaches [Topic] | Bridge to the product | One section connecting the educational content to your specific capability. Keep it proportional — this shouldn't be longer than the educational sections. |
| Related Resources | Internal links | Link to product pages, solution pages, customer stories, and related blog content |

## Tone & Style

- **Voice:** Authoritative and educational. The reader should feel like they're learning from a practitioner, not being sold to.
- **Length:** 1,800-3,500 words. Topic pages should be comprehensive enough to stand alone as the reference document on this topic.
- **Altitude:** Calibrate to the primary search audience — if practitioners search this term, write at practitioner depth. If execs search it, write for strategic context.

## Definition Block

The definition block is the single most important section for SEO and GEO. It should:

- Directly answer the core question in the first sentence
- Be 2-4 sentences total — no longer
- Include the primary keyword naturally
- Be written to function as an AI engine answer or featured snippet

Example:
> **Cybersecurity asset management** is the practice of continuously discovering, inventorying, and managing every asset in an organization's IT environment — hardware, software, cloud instances, and users — to ensure complete visibility and consistent security coverage. Organizations without complete asset visibility can't enforce security policies on devices they don't know exist, making asset management a foundational control for nearly every security framework.

## Key Components Section

This is the depth section. For each major component or dimension of the topic:

- Use a descriptive H2 or H3 that functions as a standalone query
- Define the component clearly
- Explain why it matters within the broader topic
- Use concrete examples where possible

This section should be comprehensive enough that a practitioner who reads it understands the topic well enough to have an intelligent conversation about it.

## Common Challenges Section

Don't soften this section. Real challenges, described honestly, build authority. The reader recognizes their own situation. Frame challenges as the sector would describe them — use the vocabulary of the practitioner, not the vendor.

## How [Company] Approaches [Topic]

This is the one commercial section. It should:

- Connect naturally from the challenges section
- Explain the approach, not just the product
- Avoid feature lists — focus on the methodology or architecture that addresses the challenges described above
- Include one customer proof point or metric if available
- End with a soft CTA: learn more, see a demo, or read a related customer story

Keep this section to 15-20% of total page length.

## Internal Linking Strategy

Topic pages are hubs. Link to:

- Product page (the specific product that addresses this topic)
- Solution page (the use case this topic connects to)
- 2-3 blog posts that go deeper on specific subtopics
- A customer story that demonstrates the topic in practice
- Related topic pages (other terms in the same cluster)

## SEO/GEO Optimization

- Primary keyword in H1, first sentence of definition block, and meta description
- H2/H3 headers as standalone searchable phrases ("How does X work?", "Why does X matter?", "What are the types of X?")
- Definition block optimized for AI answer extraction
- Alt text for any diagrams or images
- 150-160 character meta description that answers the core question and includes the primary keyword
- Internal links distributed throughout — not clustered at the bottom

## Quality Signals

| Criterion | Target | Red Flags |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Definition Quality | 2-4 sentence answer that stands alone as a featured snippet | Buried definition, or definition that doesn't directly answer the core question |
| Comprehensiveness | Covers all major components a practitioner would expect | Major subtopics missing — a knowledgeable reader thinks "but what about X?" |
| Commercial Balance | Educational content clearly outweighs product section | Product section longer than any educational section |
| Challenge Honesty | Common challenges described as practitioners experience them | Softened challenges that don't reflect real organizational pain |
| Internal Linking | Product, solution, story, and blog links naturally distributed | No internal links, or all links in a single "resources" footer |
| SEO Structure | H2s as searchable phrases, definition block as snippet candidate | Generic headers, keyword in H1 only |
