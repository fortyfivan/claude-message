# Partner Joint Value

Internal enablement content for alliance managers, partner account managers (PAMs), and ISMs who need to articulate the mutual business case to partners — not to end customers, but to the partner organization itself. This asset answers the partner's core question: "What's in this for us?"

## Who Uses This

- **Alliance managers** preparing for executive business reviews (EBRs) with partner leadership
- **Partner account managers** training a partner's sales team on why to lead with you
- **ISMs** positioning the alliance internally before a joint sales motion begins
- **Partner marketing** briefing partner field teams on joint GTM programs

## How This Differs from Partner Better Together Brief

The Partner Better Together brief is external — it's written for mutual customers. This asset is internal — it's written for the partner organization. The audience is the partner's executives, sellers, and alliance team, not a buyer.

| | Partner Joint Value (this asset) | Partner Better Together Brief |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Partner's internal team | Mutual customers and prospects |
| Purpose | Motivate the partner to prioritize and co-sell | Help prospects understand the joint solution |
| Frame | What the partner gains from this relationship | What the customer gains from the combined solution |
| Tone | Business case, direct, commercial | Collaborative, customer-centric, educational |

## Structure

| Section | Purpose | Guidance |
|---------|---------|----------|
| The Opportunity | Frame the business case for the partner | What does partnering with us unlock for their business — revenue, market coverage, competitive positioning? |
| Why Your Customers Need This | Connect to the partner's customer base | What problem do their customers have that this partnership addresses? |
| How the Joint Motion Works | The co-sell or co-delivery model | Specifically how reps from both companies work together — who leads, who supports, at what deal stage |
| Partner Economics | The commercial case | Deal registration, margin, co-sell incentives, MDF, or revenue share — whichever applies |
| Where We Win Together | Target use cases or customer profiles | The customer profile and scenario where joint pursuit produces the best outcomes |
| Proof | Joint wins or metrics | Pipeline generated, deals closed together, customer outcomes from joint deployments |
| How to Get Started | Activation path | Next step for the partner — training, certification, joint pipeline review, or introductory call |

## Tone & Style

- **Voice:** Direct and commercially grounded. Partners are evaluating whether to invest sales capacity in this relationship. They need the business case, not the marketing narrative.
- **Length:** 1-2 pages (600-1,000 words) or slide format (6-10 slides)
- **Altitude:** Partner leadership and field sales management — the people who allocate quota-carrying capacity

## The Opportunity Section

This section is the hook for a partner executive. It should:

- Name the market opportunity the partnership addresses (ideally with a market size or TAM reference)
- State specifically what the partner gains: new logos, expanded deal size, competitive differentiation, or access to a new buyer
- Avoid mutual benefit framing without specifics — "win-win" is not a business case

Example:
> Enterprise security teams are consolidating tooling around 3-5 core platforms. Partners who can offer complete coverage across asset visibility, endpoint protection, and SIEM have a structural advantage in procurement conversations — and are closing deals 30% larger than single-product pitches.

## Partner Economics Section

Be explicit. The partner is running a P&L calculation. If deal registration and margin details are available from `motion.md` or partner program documentation, include them. If not, frame the economics at the level available:

- Revenue per joint deal (average or range)
- Services attach rate (implementation, training, managed services)
- Co-sell program structure (who registers, how it's tracked)
- MDF availability (if applicable)
- Pipeline generation support (what we bring to joint opportunities)

If full economic detail isn't available, focus on the services and pipeline components that do have data.

## How the Joint Motion Works

Reps need to know exactly when to bring you in and what happens when they do. Frame this as a sales workflow:

1. **Trigger condition** — When does this partnership become relevant in a deal? What is the customer situation that activates the joint motion?
2. **Engagement model** — Does the partner lead and you support? Do both companies co-present? Does the partner bring you in at a specific stage?
3. **Handoff points** — What does each company own in the sales cycle?
4. **Post-close** — How does implementation and support work?

Ambiguity here kills co-sell execution. Reps who don't know the model will revert to single-company pitches.

## Example

**Input:** Write a partner joint value document for a technology integration partner who focuses on endpoint security. The target is their sales leadership ahead of a quarterly business review.

**Output:**
```markdown
# Partnering with [Company A]: The Business Case for [Partner B] Field Teams

## The Opportunity

Enterprise security teams buying endpoint protection are asking a question [Partner B] can't fully answer alone: "What assets are we actually protecting?" [Company A] answers that question. Together, you offer complete coverage — and close deals the other can't win independently.

Joint pipeline from [Partner B] + [Company A] opportunities averaged **$340K per deal** last quarter, compared to **$210K** for [Partner B]-only deals. The integration drives larger deal size because it expands the conversation beyond endpoint to full-environment visibility.

## Why Your Customers Need This

[Partner B] protects assets it knows about. [Company A] finds the ones it doesn't. The average enterprise has 15-20% more assets than their endpoint agent inventory reflects. For your customers, that gap is a compliance finding waiting to happen — or a breach.

The integration closes the loop automatically: [Company A] identifies unmanaged devices, [Partner B] deploys coverage. Customers who run both reduce their time to full coverage by 60% compared to manual processes.

## How the Joint Motion Works

**Trigger:** When a prospect asks "how do I know [Partner B] is covering everything?" or has had an audit finding related to unmanaged devices.

**Engagement:** [Partner B] rep introduces [Company A] as the asset visibility layer. [Company A] AE co-presents and runs the POC. [Partner B] maintains the primary commercial relationship.

**At close:** [Partner B] owns the endpoint contract; [Company A] owns the asset management contract. Both register in their respective systems. Post-close support is coordinated through joint Slack channel (partner scale) or dedicated joint CSM for enterprise.

## Partner Economics

- **Deal registration:** Available through [Company A]'s partner portal — [Partner B] reps register joint opportunities and receive [X]% margin on referred business
- **Services attach:** [Company A] implementation creates a professional services opportunity for [Partner B]'s PS practice — average 40 hours per enterprise deployment
- **MDF:** Available for qualified joint pipeline generation activities — contact your [Company A] PAM for details
- **Certification:** 4-hour [Company A] partner certification required to register deals — [link]

## Where We Win Together

Best-fit customer profile for joint pursuit:
- Enterprise (1,000+ endpoints)
- Has or is evaluating [Partner B] for endpoint protection
- Operates hybrid or multi-cloud environments
- Has had a recent audit finding, security incident, or compliance mandate

## Joint Wins

**[Financial Services Customer]** — 4,200 unprotected devices identified and remediated in 72 hours. Deal size: $280K combined. [Partner B] retained the renewal and upsold managed services.

**[Healthcare System]** — Compliance audit passing for first time in 3 years. Quote from CISO available for use in joint presentations.

## Getting Started

1. Complete [Company A] partner certification (4 hours) — [link]
2. Register your first joint opportunity in the partner portal
3. Contact your [Company A] PAM to schedule a joint pipeline review
```

## Quality Signals

| Criterion | Target | Red Flags |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Business Case Clarity | Partner can make the ROI case internally from this doc alone | Benefit statements without commercial specifics |
| Motion Specificity | Reps know when to bring you in and exactly what happens next | Vague "reach out to your PAM" without process detail |
| Economics Explicitness | Deal registration, margin, and services attach addressed | Economics section entirely omitted or entirely vague |
| Customer Profile Precision | Named trigger conditions and customer attributes | "Any enterprise customer" as the target |
| Joint Proof | Outcomes from actual joint deals, not hypothetical scenarios | No proof, or proof that doesn't name the joint deployment |
| Activation Path | Next step is specific and actionable (certification link, PAM contact, portal URL) | Generic "contact us to learn more" |
