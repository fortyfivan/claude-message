# Competitor: [Name]

This profile defines how to message against a specific competitive alternative — their approach, where they're strong, where they're weak, and the specific scenarios where deals are won or lost. This is a messaging tool, not a competitive intelligence database. Focus on what affects positioning, content, and sales conversations.

## Messaging Blocks

### Overview

[Instructions:
Establish the essential facts about this alternative — their scale, market position, and your strategic approach to competing with them.]

[Tips:
- Size/Stage should capture what matters competitively — funding, headcount, customer base, growth trajectory
- Strategic Approach defines how you engage: beat (head-to-head displacement), differentiate (coexist with clear distinction), cooperate (partner or integrate), target (pursue their customers specifically), monitor (watch but don't actively compete)]

[Format:
- **Category:** [their primary market category]
- **Size / Stage:** [relevant scale — funding, headcount, customer base]
- **Market Position:** [new entrant | emerging player | established player | category leader]
- **Strategic Approach:** [beat | differentiate | cooperate | target | monitor]]

### Their Approach

[Instructions:
How they position themselves — their narrative, their claimed differentiation, and the buyer problem they lead with. Write this from their perspective, not yours.]

[Tips:
- Read their website, press releases, and analyst mentions — use their language, not your reframing
- Note where their positioning has shifted recently — a repositioning signals strategic change
- Be accurate, not generous — but also not dismissive. The goal is to understand them well enough to beat them]

[Format:
2-3 paragraphs]

### Product Comparison

[Instructions:
A structured comparison across the dimensions that matter in competitive evaluations. Focus on capabilities, architecture, and experience — not feature checklists.]

[Tips:
- Dimensions should reflect what buyers actually evaluate, not what you wish they'd evaluate
- Be honest in the "They" column — understating a competitor's capabilities damages credibility when the buyer has already seen a demo
- Include dimensions where they're stronger — omitting them doesn't make them disappear in evaluation]

[Format:
| Dimension | They | We | Advantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | them / us / parity |]

### Strengths

[Instructions:
What this alternative does well. Be specific and honest — vague praise ("they have good marketing") isn't useful. Focus on capabilities, market position, or execution advantages that actually affect deal outcomes.]

[Tips:
- Every strength should be something you've seen matter in a real deal or evaluation
- If you can't name a specific scenario where this strength hurt you, it might not be a real strength]

[Format:
Bulleted list — each strength should be one specific, verifiable claim]

### Weaknesses

[Instructions:
Where this alternative falls short. Same standard — specific, verifiable, observed in real evaluations.]

[Tips:
- Weaknesses you can't prove are opinions, not ammunition
- Focus on structural weaknesses (architecture limitations, missing capabilities, slow innovation) over temporary ones (recent outage, bad quarter)]

[Format:
Bulleted list — each weakness should be one specific, verifiable claim]

### How We Win

[Instructions:
The scenarios, evaluation criteria, and buyer conditions where we consistently beat this alternative. These are the deals you want — the conditions to optimize for in targeting and messaging.]

[Tips:
- Be specific about the conditions — "we win in enterprise" is too broad; "we win in enterprise deals where the buyer has 3+ existing security tools and is consolidating" is actionable
- Connect winning scenarios to specific differentiators from space.md]

[Format:
Bulleted list of scenarios with conditions and the differentiator or advantage that drives the win]

### When They Win

[Instructions:
The scenarios where this alternative consistently beats you. Be honest — these are the deals you should either avoid or develop specific plays to counter.]

[Tips:
- If you don't know when they win, your competitive intelligence is incomplete
- Knowing where you lose is more strategically valuable than knowing where you win]

[Format:
Bulleted list of scenarios with conditions and the specific advantage they hold]

### Win/Loss Patterns

[Instructions:
Specific, recurring deal-level patterns — not general strengths and weaknesses, but observed scenarios where deals are won or lost. This is the most actionable section for sales enablement and competitive campaign content.]

[Tips:
- Draw from actual deal retrospectives, CRM notes, and sales debriefs
- The trigger is the most important field — it identifies the evaluation criteria or buying scenario that determines the outcome
- Patterns should be specific enough that a sales rep can recognize the situation mid-deal]

[Format:
For each pattern:
- **Segment:** [enterprise | mid-market | SMB | specific vertical]
- **Trigger:** [what buying scenario or evaluation criteria]
- **Outcome:** [win | loss]
- **Reason:** [specific explanation of why this trigger leads to this outcome]]

### Differentiation Messages

[Instructions:
2-3 messaging statements that frame the competitive contrast. These should derive from differentiators defined in space.md, adapted for this specific competitor.]

[Tips:
- Frame as "Unlike [alternative's approach], we [our approach]" — contrast approaches, not features
- Each message should connect to a specific weakness or win scenario above
- Do not introduce new differentiation claims here that aren't grounded in space.md]

[Format:
Numbered list of 2-3 contrast statements]

## Writing Guidelines

- This profile is a messaging tool, not a competitive intelligence database — focus on what affects content, campaigns, and sales conversations
- Be honest about competitor strengths — understating them damages credibility with buyers who've done their research
- Do not introduce competitive claims here that aren't supported elsewhere in the messaging system
- When generating competitive content (battlecards, objection handling, competitive blog posts), load this profile alongside the relevant persona profile — the competitive argument changes depending on who you're talking to
- "When They Win" scenarios should inform campaign targeting exclusions and counter-play development, not be hidden or minimized
- Strategic Approach determines engagement posture — "monitor" alternatives should not receive the same content investment as "beat" alternatives
- For status-quo and DIY alternatives, Strengths/Weaknesses frame around organizational inertia and switching cost, not product capabilities
